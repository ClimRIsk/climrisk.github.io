A new El Niño is forming in the Pacific right now.

NOAA's monitoring data shows Niño 3.4 temperatures rising ahead of the pace recorded in other recent warming years. The trajectory points toward a significant event by 2027.

Here is what that means for India — and why it matters to global markets.

The India Meteorological Department has tracked monsoon rainfall since 1871. In every moderate to strong El Niño year on that record, the same physical sequence plays out: Pacific SST anomaly rises, Walker Circulation weakens, Indian Ocean convection shifts, monsoon rainfall falls below normal. In 2009 the deficit reached 23 percent below average — the worst in 27 years. In 2015 it was 14 percent. In 2023 India imposed rice export restrictions that the FAO Rice Market Monitor recorded as pushing global prices to a 15-year high.

India supplies 40 percent of global rice trade. When India partially exits the export market, the price signal reaches every food-importing country in Asia, Africa, and the Middle East within weeks.

This article maps what happens next across six financial pathways:

- Agricultural commodity markets: rice, sugar, cotton — direct supply disruption
- Agricultural credit: the Reserve Bank of India's Financial Stability Reports document NPA ratios rising 50 to 100 basis points above trend in monsoon deficit years
- Sovereign fiscal stress: 800 million beneficiaries under India's National Food Security Act — food subsidy costs rise when production falls
- FMCG corporate earnings: rural India accounts for 35 to 40 percent of revenues for major consumer staples companies — monsoon is the single largest variable
- Hydropower and energy: the Central Electricity Authority records 25 percent of India's electricity from hydro — a deficit year forces thermal substitution
- Emerging market contagion: food-importing sovereigns from Bangladesh to Senegal face import cost spikes when India exits the rice market

And here is the part that IPCC AR6 Chapter 8 makes clear: El Niño events do not become more frequent under warming. Each one just becomes financially larger as the baseline temperature rises.

Under Current Policies, the 2040s equivalent of a moderate El Niño produces agricultural disruptions that exceed anything in the 150-year IMD record.

The physical signal is already visible. The financial models have not caught up.
