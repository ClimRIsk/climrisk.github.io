In May 2024, temperatures crossed 45°C simultaneously across Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, and Haryana.

Construction stopped before 10am. Schools shut. The NDMA issued heat advisories across eleven states.

None of it appeared in any quarterly earnings model for an Indian FMCG company, real estate developer, or agricultural lender.

That is not an oversight. It is a systematic gap.

India has more than 90% of its workforce in informal employment. No air conditioning. No paid sick leave. No financial buffer when heat forces them to stop working.

The ILO projects South Asia will lose 5.3% of all working hours annually to heat stress by 2030. Agriculture loses 8.1% at 1.5°C warming and 10.4% at 2°C. That is a structural drag on output for 600 million people whose incomes flow directly into food inflation, agricultural NPA ratios, rural FMCG revenues, and construction timelines.

The physical science is not uncertain. At wet-bulb temperature above 28°C, outdoor work capacity drops by 50%. At 35°C wet-bulb, sustained outdoor labour becomes physiologically impossible. IPCC AR6 documents that 1-in-50-year heat events become 1-in-5-year events at 2°C warming.

The financial system has not priced this.

In this piece I map the six transmission pathways from atmospheric temperature into balance sheets, with NGFS Phase 4 scenario analysis through 2050 showing what the productivity loss looks like under Net Zero, Delayed Transition, and Current Policies.
