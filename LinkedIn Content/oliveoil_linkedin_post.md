# LinkedIn Post — Olive Oil Cooperatives CRFM Article

---

Most climate risk coverage follows the same companies. Large listed multinationals with sustainability teams, TCFD reports, and dedicated ESG disclosures.

This piece goes somewhere different.

I applied the CRFM framework to European olive oil cooperatives. Not because it is an unusual choice. Because it is one of the most direct examples of physical climate risk translating into financial loss that already exists in the public record.

Spain lost 53% of its olive oil production in a single drought year. That is not a projection. That already happened in 2022.

Here is what becomes visible when you run this through a structured climate financial risk model:

• Physical hazard is already at High or Extremely High classification across the majority of Andalusia's producing basins according to WRI Aqueduct
• IPCC AR6 projects 20 to 40% less precipitation in the Mediterranean basin by mid century under moderate scenarios
• Under a Current Policies pathway, cooperative revenue falls to less than half its 2025 baseline by 2050
• The Delayed Transition scenario is the most dangerous because it removes the urgency signal without removing the physical risk
• Agricultural lenders are currently holding land-backed loans with no climate-adjusted valuation on the collateral

But more importantly, this article explains how the analysis is actually built:

• How grove-level physical hazard is mapped using WRI Aqueduct and NASA CMIP6 projections per location
• How yield reduction probability is modelled from heat and water stress events by decade
• How the four DCF adjustment terms are constructed: yield loss, water procurement cost, resilience capex, and carbon farming income
• How the spread between Net Zero 2050 and Current Policies translates into terminal asset value difference
• How the same framework that runs a multinational runs a 400-hectare cooperative

Insight: Climate financial risk does not scale with company size. The vulnerability of a small cooperative to a water stress event is structurally higher than that of a diversified multinational. The analysis should be available at the same standard.

This is part of what I am building at ClimRisk. The CRI engine takes asset coordinates, maps them to real hazard data, runs three NGFS scenarios, and delivers enterprise value and revenue impact in 48 hours. Same methodology regardless of company size.

If you work in agricultural finance, development lending, or rural credit institutions in Europe and want to understand what climate-adjusted collateral valuation actually looks like in practice, this article is for you.
