**What ClimRisk does**

ClimRisk has built the CRI (Climate Risk Intelligence) Engine — a quantitative model that translates climate scenarios directly into company-level financial outputs: enterprise value haircut, WACC uplift, FCF trajectory, and a composite CRI score (0–100, rated A–E). It covers both physical risk (floods, heat stress, cyclones, drought — 25 hazards in total at asset level) and transition risk (carbon cost trajectories, commodity demand shifts, abatement capex) under NGFS Phase 4 scenarios.

The output is not a qualitative ESG score. It is an auditable financial model: for a given company, the engine tells you what its EV looks like under Current Policies, Delayed Transition, and Net Zero 2050 — with the underlying assumptions fully traceable to IPCC AR6, WRI Aqueduct, and IEA data.

**Who it is built for — and where Aranca fits**

The model is designed for three client types that map directly to Aranca's existing base:

*Investment research teams and asset managers* who need to quantify climate risk in equity valuations. Under IFRS S2 (effective 2024) and CSRD (EU, phased 2024–2026), listed companies and their analysts are now required to disclose climate-adjusted financial impacts — not just narrative risk descriptions. The CRI Engine produces exactly the numbers those disclosures require, and the underlying model an equity analyst can interrogate.

*Private equity and M&A advisory* where climate risk is increasingly a due diligence item. Physical asset exposure (a portfolio company's manufacturing sites in flood-prone regions, for instance) can materially affect terminal value. The engine runs asset-level hazard assessments by GPS coordinate and outputs dollar losses per asset.

*Corporate clients preparing TCFD, IFRS S2, or CSRD disclosures.* The engine generates the disclosure reports directly from the model outputs — the same numbers that feed the financial analysis also populate the regulatory report.

**A concrete example**

We ran the engine on Tata Steel using publicly available filing data. Under the Net Zero 2050 scenario, the model shows an EV of -$13.2B against a Current Policies baseline of +$25.6B — a $38.8B swing driven primarily by carbon cost exposure on Indian blast furnace assets and EU ETS trajectory. The physical risk pillar identifies heat stress and water stress at the Odisha and Jamshedpur sites as the dominant hazards, contributing to the CRI score of 39.9/100 (B-rated). At 39.9, Tata Steel sits at the upper boundary of the B tier — one point below the C threshold — indicating meaningful climate-related financial exposure that is manageable under an orderly transition but deteriorates sharply under delayed or current-policy scenarios.

This is the kind of output Aranca's investment research team could attach to a sector note, or that a PE team could use as a due diligence annex.
