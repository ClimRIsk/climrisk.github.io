## The Problem With How We Talk About Climate Risk

Every year, thousands of companies publish TCFD reports. They describe governance frameworks, qualitative scenario narratives, and pages of methodology. What they rarely show is the answer to the question every CFO and every asset manager actually needs:

**What does this cost, in dollars, under each scenario?**

ESG scores tell you whether a company recycled its office waste. They don't tell you whether a steel mill is viable at $130/t carbon — which is exactly where the Net Zero 2050 pathway reaches by 2030.

This gap is not a disclosure problem. It's a pricing problem. Markets cannot price what analysts cannot quantify.

## The Findings: Which Industries Climate Policy Hits First

**The most critical metric is the break-even carbon price** — the carbon price at which a sector's EBITDA is entirely consumed by carbon costs. Below that price, the sector is viable. Above it, carbon compliance wipes out profit entirely.

**Industries already at or past their break-even point:**

- **Coal Power Generation** — breaks even at **$27/t**. Already past break-even in the EU, UK, and several Asian markets with existing carbon pricing.
- **Agriculture** — breaks even at **$33/t**. Also carries the highest physical risk score of all 21 sectors (78/100), making it doubly exposed.
- **Natural Gas Power** — breaks even at **$46/t**. Reaches break-even before NZE 2030 under every major scenario except Current Policies.

**Industries that break even within the NZE 2030 window ($130/t):**

- **Cement** — break-even at $53/t
- **Steel (Integrated)** — break-even at $54/t
- **Aluminium (Primary)** — break-even at $55/t
- **Aviation** — break-even at $71/t
- **Oil Refining** — break-even at $82/t

Under Net Zero 2050, all eight of these sectors see EBITDA completely absorbed by carbon costs by 2032. That is not a tail risk. It is the central scenario under the pathway that 140+ governments have signed onto.

**Industries with moderate exposure:**

Coal Mining, Shipping, Road Freight, and Pulp & Paper face significant compression but retain positive EBITDA under Delayed Transition scenarios — though not under NZE.

**Resilient on own-operations exposure:**

Chemicals, Oil & Gas Upstream, Real Estate, Food & Beverage, Retail, Telecoms, and Financial Services show low direct carbon cost exposure in their own operations. However, their supply chain and financed emissions exposure — Scope 3 — is a separate, larger problem.

## What This Means for Corporates

CSRD is live in the EU. IFRS S2 has been adopted across 20+ jurisdictions. Physical risk disclosure under TCFD is mandatory in the UK, Canada, and New Zealand.

All of these frameworks require quantified financial impact — not just qualitative scenarios. The days of narrative-only climate risk disclosure are ending.

If your company is in steel, cement, aviation, chemicals, or any sector above the break-even line, your next annual report needs to show investors how you survive the transition — or that you have a credible plan to get below it.
