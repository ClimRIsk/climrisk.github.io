## Paper Title

**Physical Climate Hazard Quantification in Discounted Cash Flow Valuation: A Practitioner Framework Using NGFS Phase 4 Scenarios**

---

## Abstract

Standard financial models treat physical climate risk as a qualitative overlay — a narrative addendum to financial projections rather than an input to valuation. This limits the analytical precision of climate risk disclosure and investment decision-making.

This paper presents a practitioner framework for integrating physical climate hazard directly into discounted cash flow models. The framework maps asset and supply chain geographic coordinates against three physical hazard datasets — WRI Aqueduct 4.0 water risk metrics, NASA NEX-GDDP CMIP6 precipitation and temperature projections, and NOAA ENSO cycle data — and translates observed hazard intensity into structured EBITDA adjustment terms under three NGFS Phase 4 scenarios: Net Zero 2050, Delayed Transition, and Current Policies.

We demonstrate the framework across three sectors: (1) agriculture-dependent manufacturers (rubber and tyre production with Southeast Asian supply chain exposure), (2) natural material-intensive luxury goods (Merino wool, cashmere, and silk procurement under Australian fire risk and Central Asian drought), and (3) infrastructure-sensitive businesses (Panama Canal dependence under El Niño hydrological stress). In each case, physical hazard intensity is expressed as a return-period frequency and translated to revenue and cost adjustments that flow through to enterprise value impact and credit loss probability by scenario through 2050.

We examine the 2023 to 2024 Super El Niño as a real-world validation case — an event in which a Niño 3.4 SST anomaly of +2.0°C produced commodity price impacts, infrastructure impairment, and insurance losses that were physically predictable but financially unmodelled across most affected sectors.

The framework demonstrates that physical climate risk is quantifiable, sector-specific, scenario-dependent, and financially material in ways that ESG scoring and qualitative risk disclosure approaches do not capture. The approach is consistent with TCFD physical risk disclosure requirements and compatible with IFRS S2 Climate-related Disclosures.

## What to Include as the Paper Body

SSRN accepts PDF submissions. For this working paper, compile:

1. **Introduction** — the gap between qualitative climate risk and financial valuation; the case for physical-to-financial translation (2-3 paragraphs)

2. **Physical Hazard Data Sources** — WRI Aqueduct 4.0 methodology, NASA NEX-GDDP CMIP6 models, NOAA ENSO monitoring (cite publicly available documentation)

3. **NGFS Phase 4 Scenario Descriptions** — Net Zero 2050 / Delayed Transition / Current Policies; physical implications of each through 2050

4. **The CRFM Framework** — how coordinates translate to hazard, how hazard translates to EBITDA adjustment terms, how terms roll to enterprise value and credit loss

5. **Case Applications** — rubber/tyre supply chain, luxury natural materials, Panama Canal infrastructure (draw from the existing article content)

6. **Validation: 2023-24 Super El Niño** — use the El Niño article as the validation section

7. **Implications for Disclosure and Investment Practice** — TCFD, IFRS S2 compatibility; gap in current financial modelling practice

8. **References** — NOAA, WMO, Munich Re, IPCC AR6 WGI, NGFS Phase 4, WRI Aqueduct, NASA NEX-GDDP

**Suggested total length:** 6,000 to 8,000 words (working paper format, not journal length)
