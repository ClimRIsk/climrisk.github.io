## Submission Email

Dear Enterprising Investor editorial team,

I am writing to submit a guest post for consideration: **"The Financial Anatomy of a Super El Niño: What the Physical Parameters Tell Investors."**

**What the piece argues.**
The 2023 to 2024 Super El Niño — defined by a Niño 3.4 SST anomaly of +2.0°C, placing it in the same range as the 1997 to 1998 benchmark event — produced $100 billion in insured catastrophe losses, cocoa at a 50-year nominal price high, and a 50% reduction in Panama Canal daily transits. Standard financial models did not anticipate these outcomes because they do not incorporate physical climate parameters into commodity procurement models, infrastructure valuations, or agricultural credit risk frameworks.

The piece maps the physical mechanism — Walker Circulation disruption, ITCZ shift, ENSO-driven precipitation redistribution — through to six financial transmission pathways: commodity markets, insurance and reinsurance repricing, infrastructure impairment, agricultural credit risk, emerging market sovereign stress, and equity valuation. It then draws on IPCC AR6 Chapter 3 and NGFS Phase 4 scenarios to frame the loss trajectory through 2050: El Niño events do not become more frequent, but each one becomes financially larger as the warming baseline rises.

**Why this is relevant to your readership.**
CFA charterholders who manage commodity-exposed portfolios, agricultural lenders, infrastructure investors, and EM debt analysts all carry exposure to ENSO cycle risk that is not captured in their valuation models. This piece gives them a quantitative framework for understanding why — and what physically-grounded risk quantification looks like as an alternative.

**Article details.**
Length: approximately 2,500 words.
All data from public sources: NOAA ENSO Monitoring, WMO State of Global Climate 2023, Munich Re NatCatSERVICE, Panama Canal Authority operational bulletins, ICE/CME exchange records, IPCC AR6 Working Group I Chapters 3 and 11, NGFS Phase 4 scenarios.
No affiliation disclosures required beyond author bio.
